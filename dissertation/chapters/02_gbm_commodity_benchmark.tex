\section{GBM Framework}
Geometric Brownian motion (GBM) is used as the baseline stochastic process for controlled benchmarking.

\section{Exact Discrete-Time GBM Transition}
The exact step under GBM can be written in closed form, enabling unbiased discretisation at monitoring dates.

\section{Limitations for Commodity Prices}
Although useful as a benchmark, GBM omits several empirical commodity features.

\subsection{Mean Reversion}
Commodity spot and short-maturity forward dynamics often exhibit mean-reverting behaviour.

\subsection{Jumps and Heavy Tails}
Large discontinuous moves and non-Gaussian tails are common in energy markets.

\subsection{Constant Volatility}
GBM assumes constant volatility, whereas commodity volatility is typically time-varying and regime-dependent.

\subsection{Seasonality}
Many commodity markets exhibit deterministic and stochastic seasonal patterns not represented by GBM.

\section{Brent Crude Empirical Evidence}
Existing repository analysis on Brent crude is used to contextualise where GBM is adequate and where it is insufficient empirically.

\section{Benchmark Positioning}
In this dissertation, GBM is treated as a controlled benchmark for variance-reduction comparison, not as a complete structural model of commodity prices.
